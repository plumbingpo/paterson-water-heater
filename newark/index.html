Keeping Your Newark Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Long before a tank floods a Newark garage, it usually sends signals. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, rust-colored hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it should be checked promptly. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
