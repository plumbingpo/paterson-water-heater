Upfront Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Paterson Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing on every job, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

Most water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Watch for hot water with a rusty tint, a rumbling tank, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a spent anode rod, or a worn dip tube. An early service call at the first sign usually costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of homeowners ask us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

A maintenance visit from Paterson Water Heater is more than a quick drain and refill. The technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, tests the elements and thermostats on electric units, and sets a safe water temperature. Small problems are caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and the heater keeps running at full efficiency.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, forces the burner to run longer, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your heater's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule helps. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod keeps rust from attacking the steel, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even a few years early.

Sized for Your Household

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping gallons you never use hot around the clock. Paterson Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute to the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

Households change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can overwhelm the tank that once kept up just fine. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as the flow stays within its GPM rating, while hybrid heat pump tanks draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll lay out the real trade-offs in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Repairs We Handle

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Homes in Paterson run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Paterson Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes are solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before the damage spreads. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
