How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor
  • Signs of scorching or backdrafting around the base or top of a gas heater
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater emergency packs several hazards into one closet or garage corner. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding very hot water under pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, has a reason for it. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, crank on a leaking fitting until it snaps, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into a real disaster. The safest moves for a homeowner are the ones described above: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not happen again next month. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and look for a missing or failed expansion tank. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check thermocouple or flame sensor signal, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and we say so plainly and explain the replacement path.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
