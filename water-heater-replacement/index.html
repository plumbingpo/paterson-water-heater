How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Start with the age. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. Then look at the symptoms. Water leaking from the tank, rust showing up only in the hot water, and heavy sediment noise all say the tank is close to finished. Signs You Need Water Heater Replacement in Paterson

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
