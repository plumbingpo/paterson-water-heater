Others are moving a unit out of a cramped closet and into the garage, upgrading to a larger tank for a growing household, or converting from one fuel to another. All of them mean new gas piping, vent routes, circuits, or water lines, planned out before anyone cuts a pipe. Paterson Water Heater handles installations of every size, from a single point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Good installations start on paper. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. A gas unit needs a vent path to the outside and enough combustion air. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. An electric tankless unit may need a heavy dedicated circuit and enough panel capacity to support it. A heater in an attic or above finished space needs a drain pan piped to a safe spot. We work through all of it with you in plain language, then give you a firm price.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a fast one part ways. Our technicians leak-test every gas joint, set vent pitch and clearances to the manufacturer's spec, match the circuit and breaker to the heater, and use dielectric unions or fittings where copper meets steel. We pipe the relief valve discharge full size to the right spot, install code-required expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps, and handle the permit if needed. Then the heater is filled, purged, and fired, draft and carbon monoxide are checked, and the temperature is set near 120 degrees. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required
  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch
  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • Morning showers that run cold for the last person

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Paterson Water Heater at (973) 221-6774 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Installing a water heater from scratch involves gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

Professional service handles every trade involved, not just setting the heater. Sizing by first-hour rating or flow rate, tested gas piping, a vent routed to spec, a circuit matched to the load, and the expansion tank, pan, or straps your code requires are all included. Gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check before we leave. The result is an installation that is safe, sized correctly, and ready for inspection. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • A periodic checkup with an anode inspection keeps a new tank healthy for years
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
